Getting started

The first time it opens, Sonic Rooms Live runs a short sound check: pick what you play into (a microphone or an audio interface), play something until the bar moves, then pick where you listen.

  • Use wired headphones. Through speakers, your microphone hears them and starts to ring. Bluetooth headphones add a long delay (often a fifth of a second), so you'd hear everything late: the app warns you if you pick them.
  • An audio interface with two inputs can take your instrument on one and a talk mic on the other. The talk mic only goes to the band while you hold Talk, and it's never in your recordings.
  • Change any of this later in Settings. Run the sound check again goes through it from the start.

At the top, Solo is just you, and Band is playing with others.

Solo

Solo is for practising and recording on your own. It needs no internet connection at all.

  • Arm a channel with its R button, then press record. Each input records onto its own channel.
  • Press record again to stop. Every take is kept, so try as many as you like.
  • Hear yourself (on the right of the bar with the record button) puts your instrument in your headphones as you play. It switches itself off if it hears feedback.
  • The metronome sets the tempo, beats in a bar, a count-in and its level.
  • Media holds songs and backing tracks: play one along with you, or put it on a channel.
  • Ready to share something? Copy to the band… (a take's or session's menu in the Library) copies it into your band's library.

Playing with your band

Up to 16 players can be in one room, each at home on their own computer, with a channel each.

  • With an account: make one in Settings › Account, then make a band or join one with an invite code. After that, everyone just presses Go to rehearsal in Invite, and the band's computers find each other. No codes to swap.
  • Without one: Invite shows your code. Send it to the other person and type theirs in. In the same house, choose Same house to connect over your own network.
  • Everything between you is encrypted. The sound goes straight from computer to computer: we never hear it.
  • The Room tab shows who's there and each player's delay. Green is good. If the sound breaks up, a network cable instead of Wi-Fi (at either end) usually fixes it.
  • Hold Talk (or T on the keyboard) to speak to the band on your talk mic. The Chat tab is for typing.

Recording a take together

One person leads the room: they choose the track, give out channels, and start and stop takes. Anyone can lead from the Room tab when nobody is.

  • Claim a channel for your instrument (or the leader gives you one), and arm it.
  • When the leader presses record, everyone records their own channels at full quality on their own computer.
  • Until the real files arrive, you keep what you heard of everyone else. Each part then comes across by itself, a few at a time, paused while anyone plays or records.
  • Missed a rehearsal? When you next meet, each computer asks for what it hasn't got, and it catches up.
  • The leader can Send my mix to everyone, so the band hears the same balance.

Parts recorded in a band's rehearsal stay with the band. If you leave the band, what you've already recorded stays with its members.

The library

The Library tab keeps everything in order: projects (an album, a set) hold sessions (a rehearsal or a practice, named by its date), which hold tracks (a song), which hold takes.

  • New takes go into the session and track marked recording here and new takes go here. Record new takes here on any other changes that.
  • Rename… on a project, session or track gives it a new name. In a band, everyone sees it. Takes keep their numbers (Take 3 · v1).
  • New version copies a take, so you can try a different mix without losing the first.
  • Star your favourite take of a track from its menu: one star each, and it moves if you star another. Everyone in the band sees who starred what. It's entirely optional.
  • Notes pin a comment to a moment in a take ("1:42 bass drifts here"). They show as markers on the timeline: hover to read one, click to jump there. Right-click one of yours to edit it, move it to the playhead or remove it; only the person who wrote a note can change it.
  • Open its folder shows the files on your computer. Everything is yours, in ordinary WAV files.

Mixing and playback

Mixer shows the channels as faders. Playback shows each one as a waveform on one timeline.

  • Click a channel's name for its tools: EQ and compressor, the spectrum and the master meters. The mixing never changes the recordings.
  • M and S on a channel mute it or play it alone.
  • Click the timeline or the ruler to move the playhead. With looping on, drag along the ruler to choose what to loop, or double-click a section.
  • Add a section (Verse, Chorus…) at the playhead to find your way around a song. Above the channel names, the arrows go to the previous or next section, and the section's name opens it to rename it, start it at the playhead instead, or remove it.
  • Bounce the mix makes one file as you hear it; Export stems makes one file per channel. Both go in the track's Exports folder.
  • Any panel can pop out into a window of its own, onto another screen. The Windows menu brings them back.
  • On a narrow screen, some buttons gather in the ••• menu.

Keyboard shortcuts

They work when you aren't typing in a box. F1 or ? shows the list in the app.

  • Space: play or stop (while recording: stop the take)
  • R: record, or stop recording
  • Enter or Home: back to the start
  • L: loop on or off
  • ← →: back or forward 5 seconds
  • Shift + ← →: the previous or next section
  • M: add a section at the playhead
  • N: notes on this take
  • V: Mixer or Playback view
  • Hold T: talk to the band
  • Ctrl + Shift + B: bring every popped-out window back

Lower delay

The defaults are fine to start with. For less delay, open Settings › Advanced.

  • Find the best for this computer tries smaller settings until the sound stays clean.
  • Exclusive mode gives the lowest delay on Windows; other apps go quiet meanwhile.
  • Cable test: a lead from your output to your input measures your computer's true delay.

Help and reports

  • Settings › Help › Report a problem sends us a message. We add the app's version and your version of Windows, so you don't have to. Our replies come back under My requests.
  • To report a band member, use Report to Optional Salad… in Settings › Account › Your bands › Members. To report chat messages, use Report… in the Chat tab: you tick the messages to send, and only those go. We can't read your band's chat otherwise. Nobody is told who reported them.
  • Or email support@optionalsalad.com.
